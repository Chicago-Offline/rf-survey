Directories go stale. The air doesn't lie.

Radio reference data mostly comes from aging directories and club pages — listings that outlive the systems they describe. rf-survey turns cheap SDRs into a disciplined surveying rig that checks the record against the spectrum itself: sweep, dwell, decode, and file location-tagged reception reports that validate or extend SSRF-Lite — the same data that builds codeplugs.

How it works

A declarative scan plan drives everything. Devices are claimed by serial, observations land in SQLite, and every record carries where and how it was heard.

Sweep

Wide-band power scans via rtl_power. SNR is measured against each bin's own long-term median — never a raw dB number, never another receiver's floor.

Dwell

Channels that clear the SNR threshold get a bounded-lifetime dwell: analog FM with squelch, or digital decode via dsd-fme. Supervised subprocesses, verified device release.

Decode

Color codes, talkgroups, radio IDs — parsed into structured metadata. An energy gate decides whether a decode is trusted at all, because digital decoders happily invent sync on pure noise.

Report

Human summaries, CSV/JSON export, and SSRF-Lite candidate stubs with cited observations. A human reviews before anything lands in the database — one dwell is not a system.

The validation ladder

Every system/channel pair earns a verification tier per station. Higher tiers mean higher confidence when a codeplug gets built from the data.

V0 · Heard

Energy seen above the per-channel noise median, confirmed by the activity gate during a sweep.

V1 · Decoded

Decode metadata — color code, talkgroups, NAC — consistent with the SSRF-Lite record. Contradictions flag the record for human review.

V2 · Voice

Voice activity confirmed on dwell audio, not just a carrier or data bursts.

V3 · Recorded

An archived clip backs the observation. The strongest evidence a record can carry.

A distributed survey network

Fixed stations around Chicagoland — plus mobile rigs — continuously scanning and submitting signed observation batches over MQTT.

Stations

Each install has a stable identity and an Ed25519 keypair. Enrollment is manual and small by design — a trusted set of calibrated receivers beats an open crowd of unknown ones.

Beacon calibration

Stations regularly measure shared reference signals — NWS 162.55, ATSC pilots, FM broadcast — to detect hardware drift and keep cross-station comparisons honest.

Aggregation

A central service verifies signatures, stores append-only evidence, and applies the promotion rules: verified, flagged, or stale. Policy lives in one place, not on N Raspberry Pis.

Payoff

codeplugger can filter on verification tier and location — program only channels somebody actually heard, recently, near you.

Receive-only, always. Surveyed systems are licensed to other people. Full design in NETWORK.md.

Ecosystem

rf-survey is the measurement layer. The rest of the stack turns its evidence into working radios.

SSRF-Lite

Authoritative, human-readable YAML for channel plans, repeaters, stations, and talkgroups. Where survey evidence ends up.

codeplugger

Compiles SSRF data and a profile into validated codeplugs. Verification tiers from survey data raise its confidence.

DSD-FME

The digital decoder behind DMR/P25/NXDN dwell. rf-survey orchestrates and gates it; it does the demodulation.

RTL-SDR

The hardware that makes a survey station cost less than a decent antenna. Enumerated by serial, claimed with verified release.

Chicago Offline

Resilient, community-owned communications for Chicagoland: amateur radio, GMRS/MURS, DMR, and LoRa mesh.

Quick start

Python 3.9+, rtl-sdr tools on the path, optional dsd-fme for digital dwell.

Install and list devices

pip install git+https://github.com/Chicago-Offline/rf-survey
survey devices
# 0: SN=BENCH  Realtek RTL2838UHIDIR  digital
# 1: SN=SONDE  Realtek RTL2838UHIDIR  analog

Run a scan plan

survey run examples/plan-uhf-dmr.yml --serial BENCH
survey report
survey candidates   # ssrf-lite stubs, cited evidence
